Know Before You Buy

What Makes Buying in Charleston Different

Every market has quirks. These are the ones I walk through with every buyer, because they can change your monthly payment or your renovation plans in a big way.

Flood Zones and Flood Insurance

Much of the Charleston area sits in a FEMA flood zone, and the designation can change from one street to the next. If you finance a home in a high-risk zone, your lender will require flood insurance. I request the elevation certificate when one is available and encourage you to get insurance quotes during due diligence so the cost is clear before you commit.

Property Taxes and the 4% Assessment

South Carolina assesses a primary residence at 4% of its value, compared with 6% for second homes and investment properties. That difference can noticeably lower your tax bill, but you must apply for the legal residence rate with the county. I will remind you after closing so the savings start right away.

Historic District Guidelines

Homes in Charleston's historic districts fall under the Board of Architectural Review. Exterior changes such as windows, paint colors on some buildings, additions, and fences may need approval. If you are planning a renovation, we will look into the guidelines before you make an offer.

Attorney Closings

South Carolina requires a licensed attorney to conduct real estate closings. Buyers typically choose the closing attorney, and I can recommend several who are responsive and thorough.

First-Time Buyer?

You may qualify for down payment assistance through SC Housing or other local programs. Ask me for an introduction to a lender who works with these programs often. Many first-time buyers are surprised by how little they need to get started.

Neighborhood Notes

Downtown Charleston

The peninsula offers historic single houses with side piazzas and walkability that is rare in the South. Expect smaller yards and strong long-term demand. Neighborhoods like Harleston Village and Wagener Terrace each have a distinct personality.

Mount Pleasant

Families love Mount Pleasant for its schools and newer construction. Neighborhoods range from established areas near Shem Creek to master-planned communities farther north. Traffic on Highway 17 is part of life here, so we will think about your daily routes.

West Ashley

West Ashley offers larger lots and some of the best value close to downtown. Avondale has a lively shopping and dining strip, while areas near the Ashley River include classic brick ranches that are ideal for updating.

James Island and Folly Beach

James Island blends marsh views with quick access to both downtown and the beach. Folly Beach is a laid-back island town with a surf culture all its own. Flood and wind insurance are especially important to price out here.

Daniel Island

Daniel Island is a planned community with its own downtown, parks, waterfront trails, and a mix of single-family homes and townhomes. HOA fees are part of the picture, and I will help you compare what they include.

Summerville

About 25 miles inland, Summerville is known as the Flowertown in the Pines. Buyers find more space for the money and a historic town square full of local shops. Commute times are worth testing at rush hour before you decide.

Buyer FAQs

Common Questions From Buyers

How much do I need for a down payment?

It depends on your loan. Some programs allow as little as 3% or 3.5% down, while conventional loans with 20% down avoid mortgage insurance. A local lender can show you the options side by side.

What are typical buyer closing costs in South Carolina?

Buyers usually pay for loan fees, the appraisal, title insurance, the closing attorney, prepaid taxes, and the first year of homeowners insurance. Your lender will provide a Loan Estimate early in the process so you can plan ahead.

Do I need flood insurance?

If you finance a home in a high-risk FEMA flood zone, your lender will require it. Even outside those zones, many Charleston buyers choose to carry it. We will get quotes during due diligence so there are no surprises.

How long does it take to buy a home?

Most buyers find a home within a few weeks to a few months, depending on inventory and how specific their list is. Once under contract, closing typically takes 30 to 45 days with financing.

Can I buy a home before I sell my current one?

Often, yes. Options include a contingent offer or a rent-back agreement with your buyer. I will help you compare them based on your finances and the market in each neighborhood.

Is new construction a good option?

It can be, especially in Summerville and Johns Island. Bring your own agent on your very first visit to a builder's model home so you have representation in the negotiation, and always get an independent inspection.
